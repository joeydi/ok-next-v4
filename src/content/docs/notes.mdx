Notes and case studies are MDX files in `src/content/notes/`, and the filename is the slug (`thinkmd.mdx` is `/notes/thinkmd`). `src/lib/notes.ts` reads and checks the frontmatter, the components are defined in `src/components/mdx.tsx` and registered in `src/mdx-components.tsx`, and the article's layout is the `.note-body` rules in `src/app/globals.css`. The tables on this page are read from those files and from the notes themselves.

## Frontmatter

YAML between `---` lines at the top of the file. Only `title`, `date` and `tag` are required. A missing one, a tag that isn't on the list or an `image` key that isn't in the media manifest fails the build, so a broken note never ships. Everything else is optional and only shows where it's set.

<NoteFrontmatter />

A case study adds `client`, `year` and `link` to the same fields, and they fill the sidebar's project list. A new note can start from this:

```yaml showLineNumbers
---
title: A *modern industrial* website for Rigorous
description: One or two sentences for the index, RSS and social cards.
date: 2026-10-01
tag: CASE STUDY
image: notes/rigorous/homepage.png
imageLabel: image — Rigorous homepage
client: Rigorous
year: 2024
link: https://www.rigorous.co
tools:
  - Headless WordPress
  - Next.js
draft: true
---
```

## The article

The body is one twelve-column grid, the same as `grid-12`. From `lg`, prose sits in columns 4–10 and the sidebar takes columns 1–3 beside the opening paragraphs. Figures break out to columns 2–12, or the full width, and pull quotes to 2–11. Below `lg` everything spans the full width and the sidebar comes first.

The sidebar is built from the frontmatter and the headings: "In this post", then Client, Role and Year with the site link, then Tools. Each group shows only when it has something in it, and a note with none of them has no sidebar.

## Formatting

Plain Markdown, with GFM for strikethrough and autolinks. Paragraphs are set in Hanken at `fl-20`, with `*emphasis*`, `**strong**` in ink and `inline code` on sand. Links are underlined in pink: a relative link (`/notes`, `#figure`) navigates in place, and any other opens in a new tab. A `> quote` is for a poem or a longer passage, in Gelica; end its lines with `\` to keep a poem's breaks, and give its source in a `<cite>` on its own quoted line. For one line that should stand out, use a PullQuote.

## Components

Five components are available in every note without importing. Each is a block on its own line, with a blank line before the Markdown that follows.

### Lead

`<Lead>…</Lead>` sets the opening paragraph in Gelica at `fl-30`, in ink. Use it once, first, for a sentence or two that sums up the note: it sits beside the sidebar, so a long one pushes the first figure down.

### Figure

`<Figure media="notes/<slug>/hero.jpg" caption="01 / Homepage" />` shows an image or video from the media store. Real media keeps its own aspect ratio. Silent videos loop like animated images, and ones with sound get controls. Until `media` is set, the figure is a striped placeholder showing its `label`, at the layout's ratio.

### PullQuote

`<PullQuote>…</PullQuote>` is a line from the note set large in Gelica, across columns 2–11, with `*emphasis*` in pink. Keep it to a sentence, and use one or two in a note at most. A figure just before it gives up its bottom margin to the quote's larger top one.

### TocAnchor

`<TocAnchor label="04 / Services pathway" />` adds an entry to the "In this post" list where there's no numbered figure to carry one, such as a run of paragraphs about something that has no image yet. It takes no space and is hidden from screen readers. Like numbered captions, it only counts in a note without headings.

### Profile

`<Profile media="notes/<slug>/portrait.jpg" name="Javier Heraud Pérez" dates="1942–1963">…</Profile>` introduces a person the note is about, as a margin note: a portrait, their name in Gelica, their dates in mono and a line or two about them. Put it straight after the heading of the section it belongs to. From `xl` it hangs in the right margin (columns 11–12), level with that heading, and takes no height, so keep figures and pull quotes a few paragraphs below it. Below `xl` it's a small card in the text, with the portrait beside the words. Until `media` is set, the portrait is a striped placeholder.

### Technique diagrams

A note can also have an animated diagram of its technique, such as the exploded view of the page in [Poetry in motion](/notes/poetry-in-motion). Each is specific to its note, so it isn't one of the components above: it lives in `src/components/diagrams/<note-slug>/`, and the note imports it under its frontmatter (`import { RiverDiagram } from "@/components/diagrams/poetry-in-motion/RiverDiagram";`). It's placed like a figure, with a numbered `caption`. The diagram skill (`.claude/skills/diagram/SKILL.md`) covers making one.

## In this post

The sidebar's list is built in one of two ways, never both. A note with `## headings` lists them, and ignores figures. A note without any lists every numbered caption and `TocAnchor` label (`01 / …`) in order, which suits a visual case study. Number captions in the order they appear, starting at `01`, and keep the label short: it's the link text in the sidebar.

## Publishing

A note goes into the index, the sitemap, the RSS feed at `/notes/rss.xml` and the structured data as soon as it's built without `draft: true`. Before taking the flag off:

- Upload its media with `notes/<slug>/` keys in `/admin/media`, and review the alt text there.
- Save its social card in `/admin/og`. Without it, the card goes out in the Hanken stand-in and the build warns. Re-save it when the title or its image changes: the cover, or `ogImage` when the card should show something else.
- Feature it only if it should replace the current featured note at the top of `/notes`.
