The curves are the `--ease-*` variables in the `@theme` block of `src/app/globals.css`, and every value on this page is read from there. Use them as `var(--ease-…)` in CSS or as Tailwind `ease-…` classes rather than writing a `cubic-bezier()` inline. [View transitions](/admin/docs/view-transitions) shows how the page transition combines them.

Each plot shows progress against time, with linear dashed behind it. On each track the pink dot moves with the curve and the grey dot at a constant speed, so you can compare them. Click a track to play it.

  <Curve
    ease="in-out-quart"
    title="In-out quart"
    duration="filter-move"
    used="Nav colours and background over dark sections, the nav menus and services caret (350ms), card title underlines, and notes rows sliding into place after a filter"
  >
    Holds back at both ends and does almost all of its movement in the middle. It is much steeper than the browser's own ease-in-out.
  </Curve>
  <Curve
    ease="in-out-cubic"
    title="In-out cubic"
    duration="reveal"
    used="The page transition's circular reveal on both pages, and the old page's zoom out"
  >
    A softer in-out than quart. It still starts and lands gently, but spreads more of the movement away from the middle.
  </Curve>
  <Curve
    ease="out-expo"
    title="Expo out"
    duration={500}
    used="Card lift and shadow on hover, notes rows arriving after a filter, and the new page's zoom"
  >
    A strong ease-out. Covers most of the distance right away, then spends the rest of its time settling.
  </Curve>
  <Curve
    ease="out"
    title="Ease out"
    duration="blur"
    used="The new page coming into focus, the booking dialog (300ms), and the media admin's drawer and confirm dialog"
  >
    The mirror image of ease in. Starts at full speed and slows evenly to a stop, without expo out's long tail. It replaces Tailwind's default ease-out.
  </Curve>
  <Curve
    ease="in"
    title="Ease in"
    duration="filter-out"
    used="Notes rows leaving after a filter, and the old page's blur"
  >
    Starts slowly and is at full speed when it ends, so whatever uses it accelerates away and never settles.
  </Curve>
